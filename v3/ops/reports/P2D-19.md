What I built:
- Cause (reproduced on this PC with a Character Map the probe started and closed by pid): once
  Character Map is focused it shows a tooltip over its grid, and the tooltip's drop shadow
  (class `SysShadow`, a 164x25 pane, WS_EX_TOOLWINDOW, ex style 0x800A0) is a top-level window
  of Character Map's own process, in front of the dialog. `elements` started its walk at
  `RootElement.FindFirst(Children, ProcessId = pid)`, which is the frontmost one, so it walked
  the shadow and listed nothing. `MainWindowHandle` also switched to the shadow. It depends on
  where the pointer is resting, so it was flaky. It was not timing, and not a stale `warm()` cache.
- windows.js: `candidatesScript` lists every top-level UIA element of the pid (handle, type,
  class, size), `pickWindow` (pure JS) chooses the frontmost `Window`, else the largest element
  that is not a ToolTip, Menu, MenuBar or SysShadow and has a size. `elements`, `readElement` and
  `invoke` start at `FromHandle(<chosen>)`. With no candidate, `$start` is null, so the answer is empty.
- windows.js focus: new C# `WindowOf(pid)` (frontmost visible, unowned, non-tool window) replaces
  `MainWindowHandle`. The UWP `FrameOf` fallback is unchanged.
- Cost: a pid-targeted `elements`, `read` or `invoke` now takes one extra PowerShell spawn
  (no Add-Type), about 200 ms.

Problems or doubts:
- `pickWindow` prefers a UIA `Window` over a larger `Pane`. A process with a small dialog
  Window and a big Pane-typed main window would get the dialog. That is plausible for some apps,
  but browsers are not affected: their main window is a Pane and their tooltips are excluded.
- UWP apps targeted by pid: the window lookup still finds nothing, as before (no
  FrameOf-equivalent for UIA). Out of scope here.
