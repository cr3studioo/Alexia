What I built:
- `fire`: an `At` job whose turn ends `Cancelled` is not cancelled. Its `next_at` goes back to
  its `at`, so the next `run` sees it as overdue and fires it once. It is restored only if the
  job is still listed (not cancelled by the owner). It is still recorded as "stopped".
- `run`: a map from task id to job id for in-flight turns, filled in `start` and emptied by
  `join_next_with_id`, which also covers a panicked task. `start` skips a job whose turn is in
  flight: it still moves `next_at` on and logs one `tracing::info!` with the job id.
- `run`: stops once `cancel` is set, before starting anything. Found by the restart test:
  during shutdown, `select!` could reap the stopped turn before it saw `cancel`, see the
  restored one-shot as overdue and start it again under a cancelled token.
- `run`: after starting a job, `wait` also counts that job's new `next_at`. Before, the loop
  only woke when the turn ended or at the 60 s recheck, so due times during a slow turn went
  unnoticed (and an overlap only happened on a recheck or another wake).
- The one-shot comment in `start` now says what happens when shutdown cuts the turn short.

Problems or doubts:
- Mutation check: removing the restore fails the restart test, and removing the busy check
  fails the overlap test. The owner-cancel test does NOT fail if the "read the row first" check
  is removed. `Store::jobs` hides cancelled rows, so setting `next_at` on a cancelled row has no
  visible effect. The check is there because the spec asks for it, and the test guards the
  behaviour (the job stays cancelled and is never asked again), not that one line.
- The new tests share one clock base across the two Schedulers (`base()`/`sched_on`), because
  `paused_clock()` restarts from the real wall time. With it, a restarted scheduler would not
  see the job as overdue.
- On a multi-threaded runtime, a turn could still finish in the moment between the cancel
  check and `start`. Worst case is one extra brain call that is cancelled at once and recorded
  "stopped"; the one-shot stays restored.
